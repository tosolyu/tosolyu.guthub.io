What we actually do
01

We build the technology, not just use it

In 2019 our team designed and fired up Korea's first FLASH radiotherapy research system from scratch — an experimental platform for radiation delivered in a fraction of a second.

02

We prove it in patients

Two prospective clinical trials — using gentle airway pressure to expand the lungs and steady a tumor during treatment — published in international journals, turning a clever idea into approved practice.

03

We chase the science underneath

From the tiny vesicles tumors release into the blood, to the gut microbes that may change how radiation works, our lab follows cancer down to its molecular signals.

04

We bring it home to our region

As a strategic R&D director at a national institute, our lab's PI turns frontier research into care and policy for the people of Korea's southeast (부울경) — and beyond.

Research frontiers
FLASH-RT

Can a whole course of radiation be delivered faster than a blink?

FLASH radiotherapy delivers radiation at ultra-high speed — and early evidence suggests it may spare healthy tissue in a way conventional treatment can't. We built Korea's first FLASH research platform and were first in the country to publish its preclinical effects on the blood and immune system (Radiation Research, 2025).

MICROBIOME

Do the bacteria inside us decide how well radiation works?

An in-silico and clinical program asking whether the gut and reproductive-tract microbiome — and the vesicles those bacteria shed — shape the outcome of radiation in rectal and cervical cancer. Equal parts machine learning and wet-lab biology.

LIQUID BIOPSY

What is a tumor whispering into the bloodstream?

Tumors release nanoscale packages called extracellular vesicles. By reading them from a simple blood — or even saliva — sample over the course of treatment, our team hunts for early signals of resistance, spread, and side effects.

AI & IMAGING

Can a CT scan be taught to see like an MRI?

A deep-learning project that synthesizes MRI-quality images from CT for cervical-cancer brachytherapy — so clinicians can target tumors with MRI-grade precision without the MRI. Backed by patent work and an academic-engineering partnership.

SPACE MEDICINE

What does radiation do to a body far from Earth?

Radiation biology doesn't stop at the clinic. We also work at the frontier of space medicine — studying how cosmic and ionizing radiation affect human biology, with findings headed for a leading international journal.
